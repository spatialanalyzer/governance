# Brand and Trademarks

## 1. Hexagon marks

`SpatialAnalyzer`, associated logos, `New River Kinematics`, `Hexagon`, and
other Hexagon product or company identifiers are not licensed by the
Apache-2.0 license that covers project code and documentation.

Public Hexagon documentation currently identifies **Hexagon Metrology, Inc.**
as the owner of the registered SpatialAnalyzer and New River Kinematics
trademarks. The project must confirm the exact current legal entity and
approved attribution language with Hexagon before treating that statement as
final.

Until Hexagon provides more specific language:

- describe Briosa as "developed with support from Hexagon";
- do not claim that Briosa is an official Hexagon product;
- do not claim certification, guaranteed compatibility, or official Hexagon
  support;
- do not use Hexagon or SpatialAnalyzer logos without specific approval; and
- refer trademark and product-representation questions to the Hexagon Primary
  Focal.

Hexagon has final authority over use of its marks and representations about
its products, as described in [GOVERNANCE.md](GOVERNANCE.md).

## 2. Briosa name

Ownership and trademark status of the **Briosa** name are not yet established.
No governance role or payment of project expenses, by itself, establishes
ownership of the name.

Before a trademark application is filed, the project should:

1. obtain a professional clearance search covering relevant jurisdictions and
   related software and technology goods or services;
2. decide in writing whether the mark will be owned by an individual, a legal
   project entity, or Hexagon;
3. confirm applicable employer intellectual-property and conflict policies;
4. document any license allowing the open-source project to use the mark; and
5. account for the planned Hexagon stewardship transition.

The project should not describe the name as registered or use the registered
trademark symbol unless registration supports that use. Questions about
whether to use an unregistered trademark notice should be referred to
qualified counsel.

## 3. Repository and package names

Permission to contribute code or create a fork does not automatically grant
permission to use project or Hexagon marks in a way that suggests endorsement.
A detailed community naming and logo policy has not yet been established.

## 4. No effect on open-source license

Trademark permissions are separate from copyright and patent permissions
under Apache-2.0. A recipient's right to use the code does not necessarily
include a right to use a project, product, or company name as a brand.
